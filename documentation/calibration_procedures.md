# Calibration Procedures Documentation

## Overview

This document outlines standard calibration procedures for chemical engineering instruments and equipment. Proper calibration ensures measurement accuracy, traceability, and compliance with quality standards.

## 1. pH Meter Calibration

### Equipment Required
- pH meter with electrode
- Standard buffer solutions (pH 4.00, 7.00, 10.00)
- Distilled/deionized water
- Cleaning tissues

### Procedure
1. **Preparation**
   - Allow buffer solutions to reach room temperature (25°C ± 2°C)
   - Rinse electrode with distilled water
   - Gently blot electrode dry with lint-free tissue

2. **Two-Point Calibration**
   - Immerse electrode in pH 7.00 buffer
   - Allow reading to stabilize (2-3 minutes)
   - Adjust meter to read pH 7.00
   - Rinse electrode and blot dry
   - Immerse in pH 4.00 buffer
   - Allow reading to stabilize
   - Adjust meter to read pH 4.00

3. **Verification**
   - Check calibration with third buffer (pH 10.00)
   - Reading should be within ±0.02 pH units
   - If not, repeat calibration process

### Acceptance Criteria
- Slope: 95-105% of theoretical (59.16 mV/pH at 25°C)
- Offset: ±30 mV
- Correlation coefficient: R² ≥ 0.999

## 2. Spectrophotometer Calibration

### Equipment Required
- Spectrophotometer
- Standard reference solutions
- Quartz cuvettes
- Wavelength calibration standards

### Procedure
1. **Wavelength Calibration**
   - Use holmium oxide filter or mercury lamp
   - Check peaks at 241.1, 360.9, 430.8, 536.4, 640.0 nm
   - Adjust if peaks deviate by >0.5 nm

2. **Photometric Accuracy**
   - Use NIST-traceable neutral density filters
   - Measure at 25%, 50%, and 75% transmittance
   - Compare to certified values

3. **Baseline Calibration**
   - Set blank (distilled water or solvent)
   - Zero instrument
   - Verify baseline stability

### Acceptance Criteria
- Wavelength accuracy: ±0.5 nm
- Photometric accuracy: ±0.5% T
- Stray light: <0.1% T at 220 nm
- Noise: <0.001 Abs at 500 nm

## 3. Analytical Balance Calibration

### Equipment Required
- Calibrated weights (NIST-traceable)
- Internal calibration function (if available)
- Temperature/humidity monitoring

### Procedure
1. **Daily Verification**
   - Use internal calibration (if available)
   - Verify with two certified weights
   - Record readings and compare to certified values

2. **Performance Verification**
   - Linearity check: 5 weights across range
   - Repeatability: 10 measurements of same weight
   - Eccentricity: weights placed at different positions

### Acceptance Criteria
- Linearity: ±0.1 mg (for 0.1 mg readability)
- Repeatability: ≤0.1 mg (standard deviation)
- Eccentricity: ≤0.2 mg difference
- Temperature effect: ≤0.3 mg per °C

## 4. Temperature Calibration

### Equipment Required
- Reference thermometer (NIST-traceable)
- Ice point bath
- Hot point bath
- Calibrator block

### Procedure
1. **Ice Point Check**
   - Prepare ice bath with distilled water
   - Insert reference thermometer
   - Verify reading is 0.00°C ± 0.02°C

2. **Multi-Point Calibration**
   - Check at 0°C, 50°C, 100°C, 150°C
   - Record differences from reference
   - Apply correction factors

### Acceptance Criteria
- Accuracy: ±0.1°C (for precision thermometers)
- Stability: ±0.05°C over 10 minutes
- Linearity: ±0.2°C across range

## 5. Volumetric Equipment Calibration

### Equipment Required
- Class A volumetric flasks/pipettes
- Analytical balance
- Distilled water
- Temperature monitoring

### Procedure
1. **Gravimetric Method**
   - Weigh empty flask/pipette
   - Fill to mark with distilled water
   - Weigh filled vessel
   - Calculate volume using water density

2. **Correction Factors**
   - Apply buoyancy correction
   - Apply temperature correction (water density varies with temperature)

### Acceptance Criteria
- Class A: ±0.05% for 100 mL flask
- Class B: ±0.1% for 100 mL flask
- Consistency: ≤0.02% relative standard deviation

## 6. Pressure Calibration

### Equipment Required
- Deadweight tester or pressure calibrator
- Reference pressure gauge
- Hydraulic fluid or gas supply

### Procedure
1. **Zero Check**
   - Verify zero reading with no pressure applied
   - Adjust if necessary

2. **Multi-Point Calibration**
   - Apply 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% of range
   - Record readings at each point
   - Calculate error at each point

### Acceptance Criteria
- Accuracy: ±0.1% of full scale
- Hysteresis: ≤0.05% of full scale
- Repeatability: ≤0.05% of full scale

## 7. Flow Meter Calibration

### Equipment Required
- Reference flow meter or weighing system
- Timer/stopwatch
- Collection vessel
- Fluid supply system

### Procedure
1. **Gravimetric Method**
   - Start flow through meter
   - Collect fluid for known time
   - Weigh collected fluid
   - Calculate flow rate

2. **Volumetric Method**
   - Use calibrated vessel
   - Time how long to fill
   - Calculate flow rate

### Acceptance Criteria
- Accuracy: ±1% of reading (for high-quality meters)
- Repeatability: ±0.5% of reading
- Linearity: ±1.5% across range

## 8. Calibration Documentation

### Required Records
- Calibration date and due date
- Instrument identification
- Calibration standards used
- Environmental conditions
- Raw data and calculations
- Pass/fail determination
- Calibrated by (technician signature)
- Next calibration due date

### Calibration Certificate Content
- Instrument description and serial number
- Calibration date and location
- Standards used (with traceability)
- Calibration results and uncertainty
- Statement of conformity
- Next calibration due date

## 9. Calibration Intervals

### Determining Intervals
- Initial interval: Based on manufacturer recommendation
- Adjustment based on history:
  - If consistently within specs: extend interval
  - If marginal: shorten interval
  - If out of specs: investigate and correct

### Typical Intervals
- pH meters: Monthly (or before each use)
- Spectrophotometers: Quarterly
- Analytical balances: Daily verification, annual full calibration
- Temperature probes: Monthly
- Volumetric equipment: Annually
- Pressure gauges: Annually
- Flow meters: Semi-annually

## 10. Quality Assurance

### Calibration System Requirements
- Traceability to national/international standards
- Competent personnel (trained and authorized)
- Appropriate environment (temperature, humidity, vibration)
- Proper documentation and records
- Regular review and improvement

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- Calibrating with dirty equipment
- Ignoring environmental conditions
- Not allowing equipment to stabilize
- Using expired or non-traceable standards
- Inadequate documentation
- Skipping verification steps
